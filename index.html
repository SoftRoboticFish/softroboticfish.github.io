Journal Paper · Journal of Bionic Engineering · 2025

Lateral Undulation and Force Prediction in Soft Robotic Fish: A Systematic Approach

Soft robots, as a modern gateway to unlocking the mysteries of underwater realms, present new complexities. Modeling their behavior when in contact with external forces, whether point-based or distributed, is a primary challenge due to the nature of soft bodies. To obtain a holistic view of the system’s behavior determining the governing dynamics is deemed necessary. This paper proposes a new technique to simulate the dynamic lateral undulation of a soft robotic fish with a cable-driven soft tail. By integrating the rigid finite element method with rigid-body robotics, the model represents the undulation of a finite number of rigid elements connected through a set of torsional spring and damper. Instead of directly modeling external forces, we substitute equivalent joint torques into the system dynamics, allowing us to consider external effects without complicating the model. The resulting model yields valuable insights into the system’s behavior, including propulsive and lateral forces. A comparison with experimental results shows strong agreement, with a tip amplitude error of 10% at 0.8 Hz, 5.25% at 1.6 Hz and 2.54% at 2.2 Hz flapping frequency. These findings illuminate the influence of lateral undulation on the overall dynamics, paving the way for fully autonomous robotic fish.

Conference Paper · 9th RSI International Conference on Robotics and Mechatronics (ICRoM) · 2021

Design and Implementation of a Soft Robotic Fish Based on Carangiform Fish Swimming

In the past decade, numerous underwater robots have been proposed. Nevertheless, the complexity of their designs diminishes their applicability in real-life scavenging missions. This paper proposes an optimized design of a fish-wise robot, mimicking the undulatory motion of a Carangiform fish alongside delicate performance in suboptimal tail-beat frequencies. This source of inspiration allows the robot to have high efficiency besides weight considerations. A novel traction system is embedded in the newly designed power transmission unit to exert a sinusoidal pattern of push and traction of the wires. The structure is made up of a cable-driven body connected to a compliant structure via CT sheet to eliminate joint friction and also enhance the robustness of the robot. The prototype body geometry was carefully examined through kinematic modeling. Later on, a prototype has been built and underwent experiments to verify the final results. The robot was able to accomplish an acceptable and trustable lateral motion at lower and higher designed frequencies. At the 2-hertz frequency of tail-beat, the ideal frequency, the lateral motion had a maximum error of 7%. The results were satisfactory, accomplishing the desires.

Conference Paper · ICRoM 2021

Carangiform-Inspired Cable-Driven Soft Robotic Fish

The robotic fish was developed around a hybrid body architecture that combines an actively driven cable-based section with a compliant tail. The prescribed Carangiform midline motion is imposed through the cable-driven portion, while the compliant section continues the deformation toward the caudal fin. A rotating-disk transmission converts continuous DC-motor rotation into alternating cable traction, producing the periodic lateral excitation required for body undulation without repeated reversal of the motor.

The prototype was evaluated experimentally in a water tank using image-based tracking of the body midline. The measured motion showed close agreement with the intended lateral pattern at the 2 Hz design frequency, where the maximum reported lateral-motion error was approximately 7%. Additional tests at lower and higher frequencies showed that the operating frequency directly influences the amplitude and fidelity of the resulting undulation, motivating the frequency-dependent design considerations examined in the paper.

Journal Paper · J. Bionic Engineering 2025

RFEM-Based Dynamic Modeling of Soft Robotic Fish Undulation

The dynamic model combines the Rigid Finite Element Method (RFEM) with rigid-body robotics to represent the compliant fish as a finite-dimensional multibody system. The soft sections are discretized into rigid elements coupled through torsional spring-damper elements, while their kinematics are formulated using standard serial-chain robotics. Motor-induced cable forces and hydrodynamic loads are mapped into the generalized coordinates through Jacobian-based equivalent joint torques, allowing actuation, structural compliance, and fluid loading to be incorporated within a unified dynamic formulation.

The resulting model is used to reconstruct the lateral body deformation and to estimate the propulsive and lateral forces associated with the undulatory motion. Experimental validation was performed at multiple flapping frequencies using image-based midline measurements and a six-axis force-torque sensor. The comparisons show that the model captures the principal deformation and force-generation behavior while also revealing phase differences associated with the simplified hydrodynamic representation, which are analyzed in detail in the paper.

Journal of Bionic Engineering · 2025

Can an RFEM-based dynamic model predict both lateral undulation and propulsive and lateral forces in a soft robotic fish?

The model represents the compliant fish as rigid finite elements connected through torsional spring-damper elements, with motor-induced and hydrodynamic effects introduced as Jacobian-projected equivalent joint torques. Across the tested flapping frequencies, the formulation reproduces the principal lateral-undulation behavior and provides estimates of the resulting propulsive and lateral forces. The reported caudal-fin tip amplitude errors were 10% at 0.8 Hz, 5.25% at 1.6 Hz, and 2.54% at 2.2 Hz, while force predictions were experimentally assessed at 0.8 and 1.6 Hz.

How can a cable-driven soft robotic fish reproduce Carangiform lateral undulation using a compliant tail?

The platform couples an actively cable-driven section to a compliant tail so that controlled motion in the driven section is transmitted into lateral deformation toward the caudal fin.
